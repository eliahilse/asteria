\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Researcher-written context.}
All 12 context arms of I07 and all 6 of I09 (Tables~\ref{tab:i07}
and~\ref{tab:i09}) have fewer failed issue checks than their fresh control
under every assignment of unresolved outcomes; the requirements arm has the
fewest in every cell, and functionality is not traded (I09: 40 of 40).
Review of all 79 delivered I07 artifacts: control artifacts normalise instead
of reject, clamping negative times, substituting a default name and storing
the record. Insert wording maps to code: every arm accepts negative scores
except Reuse requirements, whose insert says ``reject negative scores''. Two
checks never discriminate, since every artifact guards malformed stores and
none uses Java object serialization. The one-sentence task-only instruction
retains most of the effect (23 and 15 fewer failed checks than control).

\paragraph{Agent-acquired context.}
Six acquisitions (three angles, two methods) finished within budget in 26 to
60 commands, keeping 88 of 90 statements with 342 of 351 anchors verified.
Table~\ref{tab:agent} reports the data-flow angle. Repository access alone
leaves the counts unchanged (agentic, none) at three to five times the tool
turns. The full graph insert (about 31,000 characters) gives 3 to 10 failed
checks of 50 and, in Generation S, 1 and 2 of 5 functional. The compact
insert (12,672 and 17,051 characters) removes more failures than the I09
requirements insert (Reuse 29 to 2, Generation 37 to 3 of 50; bounds $-6.2$
to $-4.6$ and $-7.2$ to $-5.8$ per trajectory; no check increases) but costs
functional success, 5 to 3 of 5 in both cells, with submissions rising from
7 to 18 in Generation.

\paragraph{Sidecars ($N=3$ pilots).}
Adaptive slicing changes when statements arrive, not how many, because a
Highscore implementation edits the anchored methods (I11). A gate that
rejects on every positive verdict evaluated no artifact (I12); in shadow mode
the judge was right on all 6 judged first submissions (I13). Coaching after
the first submission keeps 3 of 3 functional and moves failures from 19 to
16 of 30 (I14); one rejection, or a two-turn rewind, reaches 3 failed of 30
with 0 of 3 functional (I14, I15). Only the compact insert before the first
attempt combines 3 failed of 30 with functional artifacts (I11): when
context arrives matters more than how forcefully it is applied.

\paragraph{The functional cost has one cause.}
The coupling tests end a run on a player whose team name is null; two never
set a name. An artifact passes them and \texttt{rejectsNullName} only if the
run-end hook substitutes a name before calling the store. An audit of every
final artifact's hook (Table~\ref{tab:hook}) shows that all control and all
researcher-written-insert artifacts do (``reject or safely normalize null
\ldots{} names''). The agent-written inserts say at the hook ``if the
\ldots{} name \ldots{} is unavailable, skip recording'' (Generation) or
``reject unavailable or inconsistent values'' (Reuse); 12 of 46
agent-insert artifacts follow this literally, the run is not recorded, and
exactly those two tests fail, with no other failing check. The cost is a
conflict between one insert clause and one fixture property the task
contract leaves unstated, not a capability loss.

\paragraph{Rendering without failure clauses.}
The same inserts rendered without their ``Failure behavior'' lines (I17,
I18; Table~\ref{tab:agent}) keep the effect: Generation 36 to 2 of 50 (bound
$-7.6$ to $-6.0$; no check increases), Reuse 27 to 4 (bound $-5.4$ to
$-3.6$; the oversized-line check increases from 2 to 4 of 5). Functional
success is 4 of 5 in both cells. No Generation trajectory loses a nameless
run; 1 of 5 in Reuse still does, because there the clause is part of the
control statement itself. One Generation artifact passes every functional
test and all eleven security checks, the first in that cell (two Reuse
artifacts did in I09). The clause must be
fixed at acquisition, not at rendering; the next protocol version forbids
failure behaviour that suppresses the change's primary effect.

\paragraph{Limits.}
One task, one repository pair, one acquisition per arm shared by five
trajectories; counts are descriptive. Large-record outcomes are often
unresolved (43 of 80 in I07). Artifact review and hook classes are
single-pass model judgements with cited lines; a second reader checked 12
audit rows, and the hook rule was corrected until both agreed on all 12.
