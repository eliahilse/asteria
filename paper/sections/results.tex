\paragraph{Direction.}
All 12 context arms of I07 and all 6 of I09 have fewer failed issue checks than
their fresh control under every assignment of unresolved outcomes. The
researcher-written requirements arm has the fewest in every cell. Full
functionality is not systematically traded (I09: 40 of 40).

\paragraph{Mechanisms.}
Review of all 79 delivered I07 artifacts: control artifacts normalise instead
of reject, clamping negative times, substituting a default name and storing
the record, which fails the contract. Insert wording maps to code: every arm
accepts negative scores except Reuse requirements, whose insert says
``reject negative scores''; the Generation requirements insert says ``define
bounds for score'' and all ten artifacts accept them. Every artifact guards
malformed stores and none uses Java object serialization, so two issue checks
never discriminate. Oversized-line passes come from a file-size cap before
reading or a length-prefixed binary format; length checks after
\texttt{readLine} do not prevent the allocation. All requirements and
boundaries artifacts bound retention; four operations artifacts bound at
1{,}000 instead of 100.

\paragraph{Generic instructions.}
The task-only instruction retains most of the effect (23 and 15 fewer failed
issue checks than control). The uninspected Generation S catalogue insert
reduces only retention and resource failures and leaves every input-policy
check at the control's level; the Reuse S+B acquisition inspected 22 files
and its insert reduces failures by 18.

\paragraph{Pilot: gatekeeper.}
The gatekeeper (I12, Table~\ref{tab:i12}) intervened on all 11 consultations
and no gate artifact was evaluated. In shadow mode (I13, Table~\ref{tab:i13}) it would
have intervened on all 6 judged first submissions, each failing 4 to 7 issue
checks including at least one in a category the cited statements name: the
gate fails by rejection without convergence, not by a wrong judge. Attaching
the verdict to the functional feedback (I14, Table~\ref{tab:i14}) keeps 3 of
3 functional; one rejection followed by coaching, and a rewind to before the
judged submission (I15, Table~\ref{tab:i15}; 7 of 12 post-rewind submissions
invalid, the generator having lost the turns in which it read the code),
leave 0 of 3 functional at 3 failed of 30. Only the compact static insert of I11, delivered before
the first attempt, reaches 3 failed of 30 with functional artifacts: when
context arrives matters more than how forcefully it is applied.

\paragraph{Confirmation of the compact insert.}
In Reuse S+B (Table~\ref{tab:i16}) the compact agent-acquired insert reduces
failed issue checks from 29 to 2 of 50 (bound $-6.2$ to $-4.6$ per
trajectory; six checks decrease, none increases) and functional success
from 5 to 3 of 5. The Generation S insert arm is unresolved: the provider
rejected all five requests (run identifier over its 64-character field
limit); they are retained as transport failures. Re-collected under a shorter
round name (Table~\ref{tab:i16b}), the same insert
reduces failed checks in Generation S from 37 to 3 of 50 (bound $-7.2$ to
$-5.8$; seven checks decrease, none increases) and functional success from 5
to 3 of 5; submissions rise from 7 to 18; the three remaining failures are
blank names. In both cells the compact insert removes more failures than the
I09 requirements insert (12 and 1 failed of 50) but costs functional success.

\paragraph{Where the functional cost comes from.}
A run-end hook audit of every final artifact (I09 to I16b): the coupling
tests end a run on a player whose team name is null, two never set a name,
and an artifact passes them and \texttt{rejectsNullName} only if the hook
substitutes a name before calling the store. All 48 control artifacts
and all 30 under researcher-written inserts do (``reject or safely normalize
null \ldots{} names''). The agent-acquired inserts say at the hook ``if the
\ldots{} name \ldots{} is unavailable, skip recording'' (Generation) or
``reject unavailable or inconsistent values'' (Reuse); in 12 of 46
agent-insert trajectories the final artifact follows this literally, the run
is not recorded, and exactly those two tests fail. Of the 19 agent-insert
trajectories without a functional artifact, these 12 have no other failing
check. The cost is a conflict between one insert clause and one fixture
property the task contract leaves unstated, not a capability loss.

\paragraph{Rendering without failure clauses.}
I17 (Table~\ref{tab:i17}) repeats Generation S with the insert rendered
without its eleven ``Failure behavior'' lines (15,408 characters). Failed
checks fall from 36 to 2 of 50 (bound $-7.6$ to $-6.0$; seven checks
decrease, none increases); no trajectory fails only the null-name tests, all
five insert artifacts substitute a name, and functional success is 4 of 5.
The remaining loss is a once-per-level guard reset in the wrong method. One
artifact passes every functional test and all eleven security checks, the
first in Generation S. Submissions still triple (18 against 6).

\paragraph{Cost.}
Catalogue arms reached full functionality on the first submission most often
(4 and 3 of 5); requirements arms least often (1 of 5 each). Two-thirds of I07 trajectories needed more than one submission.

\paragraph{Limits.}
One task, one repository pair, five trajectories per arm sharing one insert.
Counts are descriptive; the unresolved large-record checks (43 of 80 in I07)
limit the resource category. Reviewer classifications are single-pass model
judgements with cited lines, not double-coded annotations.
