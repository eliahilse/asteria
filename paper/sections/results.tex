\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every artifact was generated by OpenAI's \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through the
Codex adapter at reasoning effort \emph{high} (medium in the feedback
rounds of Table~\ref{tab:results}), one request per turn, no temperature
set; the same model wrote every agent document and acted as judge. Every
arm has a control collected in the same round. Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is
the baseline: the prior study's sixteen cells rerun with this generator
under its protocol. Table~\ref{tab:results} answers the question of this
paper per context strategy: does the artifact work, is it secure, and
what does it cost. Per-cell counts, unresolved outcomes and
identification bounds against each round's own control are in the
repository.

\paragraph{Functional.}
With feedback nearly every artifact works whatever the context: 29 of 30
and 39 of 40 without context, 55 of 60 with researcher-written context,
and 8 to 10 of 10 with any agent document, including the judged arms.
Under one response the three strategies deliver 10 to 12 functional
artifacts of 25 against 32 of 75 without context; a rejected delivery
(a target file returned whole, an edit anchor that does not occur) is the
main loss, and a document does not consistently add to it.

\paragraph{Secure.}
Without context the artifacts fail 61 to 73 of 100 resolved checks in
every delivery mode and none passes the five input-policy checks. Every
document lowers that: to 3, 17 and 10 failed of 100 to 139 resolved under
one response, to 18 of 93 with the compact agent document and 166 of 553
with researcher-written context under feedback, and to 12 of 92 in
agentic delivery, with 7 to 13 of 25 and 8 of 10 artifacts clean on the
input-policy checks. The residual failures are what the document's
wording leaves open, and three revisions of one instruction to the
acquisition agent show it: version 11 (bounds as numbers kept by
eviction, value domains stated) removes the retention-bound and
score-sign failures but says ``non-null'' where the check requires
``not blank'', and all ten artifacts accept a blank name; version 12
names the domain of a text, and 4 of 10 artifacts, 5 with the judge, pass
all sixteen tests and all eleven checks, where no arm before had more than
2 of 5; version 13 (bounded reads) does not help, because the artifacts
apply the byte bound after reading the line and choose record formats
whose million-record precondition the audit cannot establish. The code
graph of what the model reads changes nothing measurable; the guard
judge, cancelling at most one violating submission per trajectory with a
verbatim quote, stays within one artifact of the document alone; the
advisory judge adds submissions, not security.

\paragraph{Usage and time.}
One response reads 0.1 M input tokens and takes 1.5 to 2.1 minutes per
artifact, the document adding a tenth to a third of the time and 1 to 2 k
output tokens. A feedback trajectory reads 0.2 to 0.3 M; an agentic
trajectory 0.9 to 1.1 M in about two minutes, and 1.3 to 1.5 M in 2.5 to
3 minutes with the judge, whose calls double the count. An acquisition is
one agent run of one to four minutes: for Generation, 0.29 M input tokens
for the high-level document, 0.39 M for the full one and 0.015 M for the
generic one, which runs no command.
