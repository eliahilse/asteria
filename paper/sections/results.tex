\paragraph{Direction.}
All 12 context arms of I07 and all 6 of I09 have fewer failed issue checks than
their fresh control under every assignment of unresolved outcomes. The
researcher-written requirements arm has the fewest failed issue checks in
every cell. Full functionality is not systematically traded: I07 loses one
trajectory in three arms and gains one in one; I09 reaches 40 of 40.

\paragraph{Mechanisms.}
A review of all 79 delivered I07 artifacts, one classification per artifact
and issue check with cited lines, explains the counts. Control artifacts
normalise instead of reject: they clamp negative times, substitute a default
name and store the record, which fails the contract. Insert wording maps to
code: every arm accepts negative scores except Reuse requirements, whose insert
says ``reject negative scores''; the Generation requirements insert says
``define bounds for score'' and all ten artifacts accept them. Every artifact
guards malformed stores and none uses Java object serialization, so two issue
checks never discriminate. Oversized-line passes come from a file-size cap
before reading or a length-prefixed binary format; length checks after
\texttt{readLine} do not prevent the allocation. All requirements and
boundaries artifacts bound retention; four operations artifacts bound at
1{,}000 instead of 100.

\paragraph{Generic instructions.}
The one-sentence task-only instruction, with no security perspective, retains
most of the effect (23 and 15 fewer failed issue checks than control over five
trajectories). In Generation S the catalogue acquisition inspected nothing; its
insert reduces only retention and resource failures and leaves every
input-policy check at the control's level. In Reuse S+B it inspected 22 files
and its insert reduces failures by 18.

\paragraph{Agent acquisitions.}
Six agent acquisitions (three angles, two methods) ran on the same model
through an agent harness with a read-only sandbox (Table~\ref{tab:acq}). Every
run finished within its command budget in under five minutes. The harness
verified every kept anchor against the source; where the agent named a wrong
symbol identifier for an existing file range, the range was kept and the
correction counted. The requirements angle produced the most statements, the
catalogue angle the fewest; the two data-flow acquisitions are the static
inserts and sidecar graphs of the next round.

\paragraph{Delivery modes and sidecar.}
Table~\ref{tab:i10} reports I10: the agent-acquired data-flow graph of each
method, rendered as a static insert of about 31,000 characters, against no
context and against an adaptive sidecar that injects graph slices for the code
touched so far. Repository access alone leaves the counts unchanged (agentic,
none: 32 and 35 failed checks, 5 of 5 functional in both cells) at three to
five times the tool turns. The static graph insert gives the fewest failed
checks of any round (3 to 10 of 50) and, in Generation S, the fewest functional
trajectories (1 and 2 of 5). The file-level adaptive sidecar injected 16 to 19
of the 18 to 19 statements at the first submission, since edits touch the three
target files where the statements are anchored; its counts lie between the
static and none arms. Symbol-level slicing and a shortened insert are the next
pilots.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Catalogue arms reached full functionality on the first submission most often
(4 and 3 of 5); requirements arms least often (1 of 5 each), with the most
calls. Two-thirds of I07 trajectories needed more than one submission.

\paragraph{Limits.}
One task, one repository pair, five trajectories per arm sharing one insert.
Counts are descriptive; the unresolved large-record checks (43 of 80 in I07)
limit the resource category. Reviewer classifications are single-pass model
judgements with cited lines, not double-coded annotations.
