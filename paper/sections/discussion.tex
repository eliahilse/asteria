\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

With the final instructions, agent-written security context reduced failed
checks from 39 to 11 out of 50 in Generation and from 31 to 7 in Reuse with
every artifact functional, and two further acquisitions per task variant
reproduced the direction. The agent's context is followed literally: one
sentence about failure behaviour cost functionality until the instructions
said where rejection and where substitution belong, and an omitted record
limit moved the counts the other way. Context given before the first
attempt did better, in the pilots, than enforcement during the attempts,
and the same holds for the guard judge of the agentic rounds, whose
constrained cancellations still miss the checks the artifacts fail. Under
one response the documents keep their direction in every cell with two
compiled artifacts per arm, and the document written without the
repository is not the weakest of the three; what the wording leaves open
(the sign of a score, a record bound) is what the artifacts get wrong.
The code graph, which carries no security statement, looked like the
best addition in two small agentic rounds and is indistinguishable from
the document alone at five trajectories per cell: two and three
trajectories per arm do not rank arms in this study. What no arm reaches
is an artifact that passes every check, and the reason is in the
documents, not in the harness: neither names the contract's record bound,
and the residual failures are the ones the wording leaves open.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
One task, one pair of games, one model as agent and generator. Each arm
shares one acquired document across its three to five trajectories, so the
trajectories measure generation variability given that document; only the
final instructions were tried with three independent acquisitions. The
five input-policy checks weigh heavily in the total of ten. The
one-million-record check alone is unresolved in 43 of the 80 artifacts of
round I07. Artifact reviews and hook classifications are single-pass model
judgements with cited lines, adjudicated with a second model reader on 12
rows, not human-annotated. Only the Highscore feature has been evaluated. The provider served a
different model for some responses (3 of 50, 4 of 80, 3 of 15
trajectories in three rounds); those trajectories stopped and count as
lost, except in round I28 where a declared rule collects them again. The
judge's verdicts are counted by citation and quotation, not against a
labelled truth. The same model acts as acquisition agent and generator, and
the agent's instructions were revised after we examined failures on this
same task, so the final rounds show that the instructions work here, not
that they transfer to another feature or repository unchanged.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Repository-specific security context, appended before the model writes,
lowers the number of failed security checks in every comparison of this
study, whether a researcher or an agent wrote it. An agent's context is
followed literally, so its wording about failure behaviour decides whether
the feature keeps working; a rule that binds rejection to the operation
receiving untrusted data and substitution to its caller resolved that in
this task, with the direction reproduced by three independent acquisitions
per task variant. Next steps follow from what still fails: a required
statement about retention limits when a change persists a growing
collection, the two task cells not yet run with the final instructions, a
second feature to test transfer, and a rerun of the sidecar pilots with the
corrected context.
