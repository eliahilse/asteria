\begin{abstract}
When LLMs (Large Language Models) generate code, the security of the
output depends on what the model knows about the existing program. This
paper studies \emph{security context}: assets, trust boundaries, weakness
types, requirements and controls that a planned change touches, passed in
the prompt before the model writes. The task is a Highscore feature for a
Java game, implemented from scratch (Generation) or by reusing a sibling
game's implementation (Reuse); ten security checks and sixteen functional
tests score every artifact, and every arm is compared with a control
collected alongside it. Context written by a researcher lowers the number
of failed checks in all 18 context arms of two rounds. Context acquired by
an agent from the repository, a one-line feature description and a
vocabulary lowers it further but at first costs functionality; two
revisions of the agent's instructions remove the loss. Under one response
without feedback, three acquisition strategies fail 3, 17 and 10 checks
of 250 against 101, 77 and 94 for their controls. In agentic delivery
with feedback, a code graph of what the model reads adds nothing
measurable and a judge that cancels violating submissions is within one
artifact of the document alone at twice the calls; what decides the rest
is the document's wording: two further revisions of one rule, about
bounds and value domains, take the failures from 12 to 4 of 100 and make
4 of 10 artifacts pass every test and every check. One task, one pair of
games and two to five artifacts per arm limit what the counts can claim.
\end{abstract}
