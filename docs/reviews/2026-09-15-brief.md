# Review brief

You are reviewing a short research paper that its author will read aloud to two supervisors tomorrow. Files in this directory:

- `paper.txt`: the paper as extracted from the built PDF (five pages, the last two mostly references).
- `paper-sources.tex`: the LaTeX sources of the four sections, in order, with the tables.
- `supervisors-notes.md`: what the supervisors asked for in the previous meeting. The paper is the answer to those asks.

Read all three completely before writing anything. Do not modify any file.

Review as a careful, skeptical reader who knows software security and empirical software engineering but has never seen this project. Your standard: every sentence must be understandable on first hearing, every number must say what it counts and out of what, every claim must be backed by a table or a stated observation, and there must be no filler.

Produce `REVIEW.md` in this directory with exactly these sections:

1. **Unexplained or confusing numbers.** Quote each number or range that a listener could not interpret on first hearing (what is it, out of what, which arm, which round), and say what is missing.
2. **Unexplained or invented terms.** Quote each term used before it is defined, or defined vaguely, or that sounds invented rather than standard. Say where a standard term exists.
3. **Claims not backed by the tables or by a stated observation.** Quote the sentence and say what evidence is missing or contradicts it. Check the prose against Tables 2 to 5: recompute any difference the prose states from the table cells and flag mismatches.
4. **Where the chain of reasoning breaks.** The results section is written as steps. For each step, say whether the question it answers follows from the previous step's result and whether the step's conclusion is actually supported.
5. **Bloat.** Quote sentences or clauses that carry no information a listener needs, or repeat something already said.
6. **Scientific accuracy and honesty.** Anything overstated, understated, or missing (limits, confounds, alternative explanations). Be concrete.
7. **Supervisor asks.** For each ask in `supervisors-notes.md`, say whether the paper visibly answers it, and quote where; if not, say what one sentence would.
8. **Three highest-value fixes.** The three edits that would most improve the paper, each as: quoted original, proposed replacement.

Rules: quote the paper verbatim for every point; do not invent facts about the study; if something is ambiguous, say what it could mean rather than guessing; keep each point to at most three sentences; do not praise; do not summarize the paper back to me.
