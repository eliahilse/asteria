## 1. Unexplained or confusing numbers.

- **“all 18 arms of two rounds”** — Name the rounds and explain the count: twelve context conditions across four cells in I07, plus six across two cells in I09, excluding controls. Otherwise, a listener cannot connect eighteen arms to the three context types introduced later.

- **“An audit of 124 artifacts”** — Table 5 sums to 124, but Tables 3 and 4 display only 100 trajectories for the covered rounds: 40 controls, 30 researcher-context trajectories and 30 agent-context trajectories. Identify the additional eight controls and sixteen agent-context trajectories, including their rounds and conditions.

- **“Reading all 79 delivered artifacts”** and **“43 of 80 artifacts in round I07”** — Table 2 contains eighty trajectories, but the relationship between eighty trajectories and seventy-nine delivered artifacts is unexplained. State what happened to the remaining trajectory and how it contributes to the functional and security denominators.

- **“failed checks fall from 39 to 11 and from 31 to 7 out of 50”** — The abstract does not identify I20, associate the first pair with Generation and the second with Reuse, or explain that fifty means ten checks on each of five artifacts. These are repeated check outcomes, not fifty distinct security issues.

- **“two further acquisitions reproduce the result”** and **“five artifacts per arm”** — The later experiments use two further acquisitions **per task variant**, with three trajectories per arm. The abstract conceals that distinction and gives an incorrect blanket sample size.

- **“may resubmit up to five times”** and **“within the five submissions”** — These describe different budgets: an initial submission plus five retries, or five submissions altogether. State the actual total, including the initial attempt.

- **“the ten security checks of Table 1, plus a persistence round trip”** — Explain whether the round trip is an eleventh reported check, a functional test, or a prerequisite for interpreting the ten security checks. Its result and effect on the denominator are absent.

- **“a 64 MiB heap and a 15 s limit”** — State whether each check receives a fresh JVM and limit, or whether the entire artifact evaluation shares them. Explain how exceeding either limit is classified.

- **“between 4.6 and 5.8 failed checks per artifact”** — The reporting example has no named round or task variant. Those endpoints match I20 Generation, whereas the later I20 range of 4.4–5.8 combines Generation and Reuse.

- **“using 26 to 60 commands”** — The actual command budgets are not given. State which acquisition used each endpoint and whether the budgets were identical across acquisition approaches and task variants.

- **“the harness kept 88 of the 90 statements produced and verified 342 of the 351 code locations they cite”** — State explicitly whether these totals cover the six initial documents and whether locations are unique locations or citation occurrences. Explain whether the nine unresolved citations belonged entirely to the two discarded statements.

- **“about 31,000 characters”** — Identify whether this is one document’s length, an average, or an approximation covering both task variants. Give the compact document lengths if document size is part of the explanation.

- **“3 to 10 failed checks out of 50”** and **“only 1 and 2 artifacts out of 5 are functional”** — These pool different delivery modes and task variants. In I10 Generation, the single-shot insert has five failed checks and two functional artifacts, while the agentic insert has three failed checks and one functional artifact.

- **“at three to five times the tool calls”** — Neither the underlying call counts nor the aggregation unit appears. State whether these are totals or averages per trajectory and which calls are included.

- **“18 submissions instead of 7 in Generation”** — Identify I16b, label eighteen as the insert-arm total and seven as the control-arm total, and say that each total covers five trajectories. Also clarify whether “submissions” includes the initial attempt.

- **“all 6 first submissions it flagged”** — State how six flagged first submissions arise from experiments with three trajectories per arm, including which conditions were pooled. Give the number inspected and the method used to decide that each flag was correct.

- **“Advice keeps 3 of 3 artifacts functional but lowers failed checks only from 19 to 16 out of 30”** — The stated reduction is three check failures, but the task variant, round and unresolved counts are missing. The accompanying **“3 failed out of 30 with functional artifacts”** also needs the exact functional count and the control for that condition.

- **“checked 12 classified rows”** — State twelve out of how many eligible classifications, how the rows were selected, and whether these are the same twelve artifacts classified as failing only the nameless-player tests.

- **“10 and 10; 11 and 13”** — Label both pairs with the task variant and the insert/control order. If these are totals over five trajectories, say so rather than requiring the listener to infer it.

- **“2 or 3 artifacts out of 3 to 5”** — Give the three exact Reuse results for I20, I21a and I21b. Combining numerator and denominator ranges prevents the listener from recovering any individual failure rate.

- **“from about half with a generic sentence to nine in ten with the best inserts”** — Identify the selected arms and whether the denominator is observed control failures or all scheduled checks. The generic reductions are 23 of 38 and 15 of 31 observed failures; the final I20 reductions are 28 of 39 and 24 of 31, so “nine in ten” is not the final protocol’s result in that round.

## 2. Unexplained or invented terms.

- **“a short, typed description”** and **“the set of typed statements”** — Explain that “typed” means statements labelled as assets, requirements, controls and other categories, if that is the intended meaning. It could otherwise suggest programming-language types or a formally checked representation.

- **“assets”** — The abstract introduces this without an example or definition. Identify something being protected in this task, such as stored score records, and connect it to the relevant property.

- **“A threat is a way that property could be violated”** — “A way” blurs a threat with an attack method or scenario. The cited glossary describes a threat as a potential security violation arising from circumstances, entities or events that could cause harm; use that distinction. [RFC 4949](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4949.html)

- **“parts of a system that trust each other differently”** — This does not explain what differs: permissions, assumptions about input validity, or authority. Describe the actual boundary between the caller and score store, including what each may assume about incoming data.

- **“a control is the mechanism that guarantees it”** — “Guarantees” turns an intended safeguard into an established assurance. Use “a mechanism intended to enforce the requirement” unless the guarantee has actually been demonstrated.

- **“STRIDE”**, **“CAPEC”**, **“CWE”**, **“ASVS”** and **“CERT IDS”**, **“CERT ERR”**, **“CERT MSC, FIO”**, **“CERT SER”** — These are recognized names, but their expansions or operational meanings are not consistently supplied. Explain the catalogues and standard once, and replace opaque rule-family abbreviations with named rules where they support a particular check.

- **“An arm is one security-context condition”** and **“All twelve context arms”** — The definition suggests three researcher-context arms, while the results count each condition within each cell separately. Define an arm as a context condition within a specified task, base-context configuration and delivery mode, or consistently call these twelve comparisons.

- **“structural, functional or behavioural descriptions, marked S, F, B”** — The distinction between functional and behavioural descriptions is unspecified. State whether these are source-file selections, generated summaries or manually written documents, and provide one concrete example of each.

- **“single-shot”** — The model can receive compiler and functional-test feedback and submit repeatedly, so this does not describe a single attempt. Use “without repository-search tools” and “with repository-search tools” for the distinction actually described.

- **“insert”**, **“compact insert”** and **“full graph”** — “Insert” appears before its explicit explanation, and “full graph” appears without any definition of nodes or edges. “Additional prompt text,” “requirements and controls only,” and “full context document” would express the reported distinctions without implying an unexplained graph representation.

- **“operations”** and **“requirements”** — Their definitions both describe constraints on feature operations, leaving the experimental difference unclear. Show one operation expressed under each condition and identify exactly what information or wording changes.

- **“Task only”** and **“Catalogue”** — Every arm already receives the task, so “Task only” is misleading for an arm that adds a generic security sentence. “Catalogue” also names both a flat CWE list and an agent procedure that evaluates applicability; distinguish those interventions.

- **“Agent-acquired inserts, slices”** — “Slices” is not defined anywhere. State which document subsets or experimental conditions it denotes, particularly because this group contains trajectories absent from the other tables.

- **“sidecar”**, **“one-turn judge”** and **“rewinds the conversation two turns”** — Explain these as an auxiliary monitor, a model-based evaluator, and a specific conversation-editing operation. Specify whether a turn means one message or a complete model/tool exchange, and whether rewinding also restores edited repository files.

- **“coupling tests”** and **“whose name is null because it has no AI”** — “Integration tests” is the familiar term if these tests exercise the game-to-store connection. Explain what “AI” means inside the game and why its absence leaves the test player unnamed.

- **“reject”**, **“safely normalize”** and **“skip recording”** — These are not interchangeable behaviours, and their exact meanings determine the central result. State whether rejection means throwing an exception, returning an error, or declining insertion, and name the operation at which each behaviour occurs.

- **“protocol v9”** and **“protocol v10”** — These appear to be revisions of the acquisition agent’s instructions, rather than communication protocols. Label them as instruction revisions and give the changed rule alongside each label.

## 3. Claims not backed by the tables or by a stated observation.

- **“Functionality is not traded away: the control arms have 4 or 5 functional artifacts out of 5, the context arms likewise.”** Table 2 shows three decreases from five functional artifacts to four: Generation S with Operations, Generation S with Boundaries, and Generation S+F+B with Requirements. The abstract’s **“at no cost in functionality”** repeats this overstatement; equal ranges across groups do not establish equal outcomes within comparisons.

- **“All twelve context arms fail fewer security checks than their control, whichever way the unresolved checks are counted; the requirements insert does best in every cell.”** All twelve control comparisons retain a positive reduction under worst-case unresolved outcomes, but the Requirements ranking is only assured for observed failures. If the uncertainty qualification also applies to “does best,” Reuse B is a counterexample: Requirements could have six failures after resolving its two unknowns, while Operations could remain at five.

- **“Letting the model read the repository on its own, without context, changes nothing: 32 and 35 failed checks out of 50 against 33 and 35 for single-shot controls, at three to five times the tool calls.”** Table 3 shows one fewer observed failure in Generation and no difference in Reuse, with unresolved counts also changing. These small samples do not establish equivalence, and the tool-call multiplier cannot be checked from any table.

- **“The agent's full document as an insert, about 31,000 characters, gives 3 to 10 failed checks out of 50, the fewest so far, but in Generation only 1 and 2 artifacts out of 5 are functional.”** “The fewest so far” contradicts the displayed earlier results: I07 Reuse S+B Requirements has zero observed failures, and I09 Reuse Requirements has one. Restrict the comparison to a named, comparable condition or remove the superlative.

- **“So the agent's context is stronger than the researcher's and costs something the researcher's did not.”** The paper does not report a contemporaneous comparison isolating context authorship while holding content, length and delivery constant. The functionality clause also conflicts with the three researcher-context decreases in Table 2.

- **“Injecting on demand changes when statements arrive, not how many, because the feature touches the very methods they cite.”** Touching cited methods does not establish equal delivered statement counts, repetition or total prompt length. Supply the delivery-log observation that establishes this equality.

- **“When context arrives matters more than how hard it is enforced; the rest of the study is single-shot.”** The pilot changes timing, document selection, feedback and rejection or rewind behaviour without reporting a design that isolates their effects. It supports describing the observed pilot outcomes, not ranking timing above enforcement as a general cause.

- **“Rendering cannot fix what the agent decided.”** Removing a designated field leaves a problematic sentence elsewhere in the Reuse document, which shows that this particular filter missed it. It does not show that editing or filtering the actual control statement could not fix the document.

- **“The lesson is that the effect of every earlier insert was carried by the word ‘reject’ at the store.”** No experiment changes only that word while holding the rest of the context fixed. The reported observations identify rejection placement as one mechanism, without attributing every earlier check improvement to it.

- **“The cost is one sentence the agent wrote colliding with one property of the test fixture, not a loss of capability.”** Table 5 identifies twelve agent-context artifacts with one specific failure pattern, but does not establish that this accounts for all functional failures. I17 Generation and I19 Reuse still have only four functional artifacts out of five despite the stated absence of lost nameless runs.

- **“under every way of counting the unresolved checks, from all-pass to all-fail”** needs an explicit calculation rule. For equal arm size \(N\), control failures/unresolved outcomes \(F_c,U_c\), and insert failures/unresolved outcomes \(F_i,U_i\), the reduction bounds are \([(F_c-F_i-U_i)/N,\ (F_c+U_c-F_i)/N]\); assigning the same outcome to all unknowns in both arms does not cover every possibility.

## 4. Where the chain of reasoning breaks.

- **Step 1 — “does context help at all?”** This is the opening question, and the reductions against controls support an effect on the selected security-check outcomes. The step overextends that result to no functional cost, and I09 does not isolate repository knowledge because specificity, wording and amount of instruction also differ.

- **Step 2 — “can an agent write it?”** This follows from the concern that a knowledgeable researcher supplies the useful context, and the generated documents plus lower check-failure counts address feasibility. It does not establish superiority over researcher-written context, and selecting the data-flow approach because it cites more code does not establish that it produces more accurate or useful guidance.

- **Step 3 — “timing or force?”** This changes the question after Step 2 exposes lost functionality, without explaining why delivery timing or enforcement should cause that loss. The pilot can guide a delivery choice, but its reported conditions do not support the conclusion that timing matters more than enforcement.

- **Step 4 — “where the cost comes from.”** This returns to the unresolved question from Step 2 rather than following from Step 3’s result. The hook inspection and test failures support a specific compatibility problem in twelve artifacts, but do not explain every functional failure or establish that one sentence is the sole cause.

- **Step 5 — “removing the sentence by rendering.”** This follows from the hook diagnosis, but the Reuse intervention does not actually remove the sentence because it is stored in another field. Generation’s four-of-five functional result also leaves a separate failure unexplained, so the conclusion should describe the limited filter’s outcome.

- **Step 6 — “removing it in the agent's instructions.”** This follows from the need to change the generated guidance and supports testing an explicit caller/store distinction. Fresh acquisitions change more than one sentence, however, so the results support repeated improvement on this task without proving that one word carried every earlier effect or that the instructions generalize.

## 6. Scientific accuracy and honesty.

- **“the contract counts that as a failure because it asks for rejection”** — This makes contract compliance a major part of the security outcome, but does not establish that every normalization is insecure. CWE-20 explicitly permits rejecting invalid input or transforming it into acceptable input, so distinguish this study’s rejection policy from a general security requirement. [CWE-20](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/20.html)

- **“Each check probes a weakness type, not an exploit.”** The later claims about security need to retain this qualification. Five of the ten checks concern input policy, so the aggregate weights that policy heavily and cannot be interpreted as a count of independent vulnerabilities or an estimate of overall security.

- **“does the code deserialize Java objects, which would let a crafted file execute code”** — Deserialization alone does not establish that arbitrary code execution is possible. The outcome depends on untrusted input reaching deserialization, available classes and methods, and applicable restrictions; describe a potentially dangerous mechanism unless an exploit path was demonstrated. [CWE-502](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/502.html)

- **“trusts files that should be checked”** — The paper does not state the attacker’s access, the origin of hostile files, or whose integrity and availability are threatened. Define the threat model so a listener can distinguish a security boundary from robustness against a player corrupting their own local data.

- **“the feature contract, the OWASP ASVS verification standard, or the CERT Java secure coding rules”** — Table 1 gives broad sources rather than a reproducible mapping from each test to its requirement. Supply exact rule identifiers and the missing test parameters, including the permitted name length and huge-line size, and distinguish study-selected thresholds from standard requirements.

- **“It receives no test, no threshold, no earlier context and no generated code.”** This describes the acquisition agent’s direct inputs, but the researchers revise its instructions after examining failures from the same task and test suite. Disclose that feedback path so the final acquisitions are not mistaken for evaluation on an untouched task.

- **“sees only the repository and a one-line feature description”** — The abstract omits the supplied schema, security identifiers and revised acquisition instructions. These inputs matter because the paper ultimately attributes the result to the instructions.

- **“the harness checks that each citation exists, records its hash, and drops any statement whose citation does not resolve”** — Resolvable citations establish that referenced code exists, not that it supports the statement or that the proposed control is appropriate. Distinguish citation validation from semantic validation of the acquired guidance.

- **“A safe and a deliberately weak reference implementation must produce the expected outcomes before every run, so a check that could never fail would be caught.”** These references show that the harness distinguishes those implementations, not that it correctly handles every generated format or implementation. Explain how adapters, unmet preconditions and the persistence round trip prevent misleading passes on incomplete or nonfunctional code.

- **“The large-record check is often unresolved (43 of 80 artifacts in round I07).”** Table 2 contains sixty-one unresolved check outcomes in total, leaving eighteen beyond those forty-three that need accounting. File-format-dependent evaluability can vary with the intervention, so report unresolved outcomes by check and reason rather than treating them as a single interchangeable category.

- **“A second, independent model reader checked 12 classified rows and the rule was refined until both agreed.”** Agreement after revising the rule is adjudicated agreement, not an independent estimate of classification reliability. Report row selection, initial disagreements, reader blinding and whether the revised rule was reapplied to the entire audited set.

- **“Table 3 follows the data-flow angle, which cites the most code.”** State whether this selection rule was chosen before observing generated-code outcomes and what happened to the other two acquisition approaches. More citations are not evidence of better security coverage.

- **“the result belongs to the instructions, not to one lucky document”** — Three acquisitions per task variant provide repeated observations on the same task and repository pair. They do not isolate instruction changes from other changes in the acquired documents or establish reliability on new features.

- **“Each arm shares one acquired document across its three to five trajectories”** — The trajectories measure generation variability conditional on that document; they are not independent samples of acquisition quality. The unresolved-outcome bounds also exclude sampling variation, so they cannot support population-level precision.

- **“one model as agent and generator”** — The model’s identifier and version are missing, as are the actual sampling settings and sufficient acquisition and generation budgets. Repository revisions, complete prompts, test implementations and execution ordering are also needed to reproduce the reported comparisons.

- **“the operation that receives untrusted data rejects and never substitutes; the caller substitutes before calling it”** — This is a rule tailored to the stated caller/store contract, not a universal secure-coding rule. Explain why this caller may supply a default name and which invalid inputs still require rejection.

- **“under every version 10 Reuse insert”** and **“none of those inserts mentions a record limit while the earlier insert did”** — The final protocol repeatedly introduces a measured retention-policy regression. Treat omitted requirements as a limitation of acquisition coverage and report the exact counts alongside the successful input-policy changes.

- **“users assisted without security cues write less secure code”** — The comparator is missing, and the wording implies evidence about the effect of security cues. The cited study’s principal comparison was access to an AI assistant versus no access, so name that comparison rather than attributing the difference to missing cues. [Perry et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622)

- **“where flaws travel with the copies”** — The paper does not identify a particular ApoIcarus flaw and show that a generated Reuse artifact inherited it. Present this as a motivating risk unless a donor-to-generated-code trace is supplied.

## 8. Three highest-value fixes.

1. **Remove the false claim of no functional cost.**

   **Original:** “Context written by a researcher lowers the number of failed security checks in all 18 arms of two rounds at no cost in functionality.”

   **Proposed replacement:** “Researcher-written context reduced failed security checks in all twelve comparisons in I07 and all six in I09. In three I07 comparisons, four of five context artifacts were functional, compared with five of five control artifacts.”

2. **Limit the causal explanation to the failure pattern actually identified.**

   **Original:** “The cost is one sentence the agent wrote colliding with one property of the test fixture, not a loss of capability.”

   **Proposed replacement:** “In twelve of forty-six agent-context artifacts, the code omitted a nameless player's run and failed only the two tests requiring that run to be recorded. This identifies one functional failure pattern; it does not explain all functional failures.”

3. **Replace the broad conclusion with the final result and its recurring regression.**

   **Original:** “Repository-specific security context removes most of the security failures a control produces, from about half with a generic sentence to nine in ten with the best inserts, whether a researcher or an agent writes it.”

   **Proposed replacement:** “In I20, the final agent instructions reduced observed failed checks from 39 to 11 in Generation and from 31 to 7 in Reuse, out of fifty checks per arm, with all five artifacts in each arm functional. Two further acquisitions per task variant, tested with three artifacts per arm, again reduced failures with every artifact functional, but all three version 10 Reuse contexts increased failures of the hundred-record-limit check.”
